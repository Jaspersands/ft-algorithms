1Fault-tolerant simulation in your browser

Below, the engine of this project, compiled to WebAssembly, runs quantum algorithms on a simulated surface-code machine: Shor order finding (textbook vs. modern windowed + MBU arithmetic) and molecular chemistry (H₂ ground-state phase estimation). Every operation carries its calibrated logical error channel. Pick a distance, physical noise, and magic-state factory, then press run: below threshold outcomes smear into noise; above it they peak sharply at the exact solution.

Why this is legitimate: with Pauli noise, a Clifford circuit and a Pauli-frame decoder, the logical action of one surface-code operation is a Pauli channel (every syndrome history ends in some logical Pauli). The single approximation is to compose consecutive operations' channels as independent. Section 4 measures how good that is.

5Measuring the machine

The idle error of a patch per round of syndrome extraction falls exponentially in the distance d, by a factor Λ per two units of distance. At p = 0.1% the fit gives Λ ≈ 11; at 0.5%, close to threshold, barely more than one.

Lattice surgery adds its own errors. A Z⊗Z merge can report the wrong outcome; a CNOT (two merges and an ancilla patch, 2d rounds) can leave errors on control and target. Extracting an operation's own channel needs care: errors from the rounds before the gate pass through it (a CNOT copies an X error on the control onto the target), so the baseline is pushed through the ideal operation before it is divided out. Done naively, the CNOT's “X on the control only” rate comes out negative.

A reference-qubit experiment (the patch entangled with a perfect qubit by noiseless Pauli-product measurements before any noise, read jointly at the end) gives the full single-qubit channel, including logical Y errors, which X- and Z-basis memories cannot see separately. Y is rare but not negligible at small distance: about 5% of the X rate at d = 3 and p = 0.1%, several times more than independent X and Z errors would give.

Checked against Stim and PyMatching

A subset of the experiments rerun with Stim's sampler and PyMatching's correlated matching agree with stabilizer-qec within statistics: all within 3σ.

6Do the parts add up?

Three whole lattice-surgery experiments were rebuilt as logical programs from their calibrated parts (half-memories before and after, each merge's measured channel, idling of patches not in a merge) and simulated by ftsim, then compared with the circuit-level measurement of the same experiment: the CNOT (from a Z⊗Z and an X⊗X merge), three Z⊗Z measurements in a row, and three patches merged at once.

The composed prediction of each experiment's failure rate is within 0.93–1.26× the measurement, median 1.05×: the independent-composition model is accurate to about 10% and errs on the pessimistic side. The algorithm results below inherit that margin.

7Shor's algorithm

The order-finding circuit is made of honest parts: modular addition of a classical constant with a sign flag (Beauregard), controlled modular multiplication with uncomputation by the inverse, and one recycled control qubit read out through a semiclassical approximate QFT whose correction rotations are synthesized into Clifford + T. Every multiplier is the generic circuit, even when its classical constant happens to be 1, so nothing in the circuit uses the period it is meant to find: no “compiled Shor”. Every arithmetic block was verified on every input by an independent bit-level simulator.

Why not score runs by whether they find the factors? Because for small N almost every outcome does: with the standard classical post-processing, a uniformly random outcome factors 15 with probability 0.96 and 21 with 0.999, the trap Smolin, Smith and Vargo pointed out. The score used here is the peak probability: the share of runs whose outcome is one of the r ideal peaks. Its random baseline is r/2m, below 1%.

Modern arithmetic: measurement-based uncomputation & windowing

Replacing textbook reversible adders with Gidney (2018) carry uncomputation (transversal Hadamard, mid-circuit X measurement, patch reset, and classical feed-forward CZ fixup) cuts Toffolis in half per adder with zero ancilla overhead. Which magic states, and what fails

Each Toffoli consumes a |CCZ⟩ state and each rotation's T gates a |T⟩ state. Their error sets a floor no distance can lower: with injected states (error ≈ p, distilled once into |CCZ⟩) the peak probability of N = 35 saturates at —; distilled and cultivated states lower the floor below what the distances shown can resolve.

8Molecular energies by phase estimation

The Hamiltonians are computed from scratch: STO-3G integrals in closed form, restricted Hartree–Fock, Jordan–Wigner and Z₂ tapering, checked against PySCF to 10⁻⁸ hartree. Iterative phase estimation then reads the ground-state energy from 10 bits of a fourth-order Trotterized e−i(H−EHF)τ, each controlled rotation synthesized to Clifford + T. Reaching chemical accuracy (1.6 mHa) takes 1.8×10⁷ T gates for H₂ alone.

With 1.8×10⁷ T gates, even cultivated magic states at 2×10⁻⁹ † leave a floor of a few percent: distance cannot fix the factory.

Scaling to multi-electron chemistry: Lithium Hydride (LiH)

Beyond minimal diatomics, Lithium Hydride (LiH) features 4 electrons across 12 spin orbitals in STO-3G. Freezing the Li 1s² core and tapering Z₂ spin and parity symmetries yields a 2-qubit, 9-term Hamiltonian matching PySCF CASCI to < 10⁻¹⁴ Ha across the dissociation curve (equilibrium at R = 1.595 Å, E = −7.882 Ha). 10-bit QPE requires 4.19 × 10⁷ T gates. 9To cryptographic and chemical scale

The same measured error model extrapolates. For textbook and modern circuits the gate counts are exact functions of the modulus size and the schedule shape is fitted on compiled instances; for published algorithms, counts and run time come from the papers and only the error model is ours.

Headline sensitivity: why Gidney 2025 needs d = 29 under measured noise

Compiling Gidney's 2025 RSA-2048 counts under our calibrated SD6 error model requires d = 29 (5.31 M physical qubits), whereas Gidney reported d = 25 (898 k physical qubits). Our multi-parameter sensitivity campaign shows this is not a modeling artifact: at d = 25, circuit-level SD6 noise decodes to an idle logical error rate 4.3× higher than Gidney's uncalibrated assumption (4.30 × 10⁻¹⁵ measured vs. 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ assumed). Feeding Gidney's assumed rate into our compiler reproduces d = 25.

Biased noise & XZZX surface codes: 3.4×–4.25× footprint reduction

In architectures where phase flips dominate bit flips (such as dual-rail superconducting qubits or Kerr-cat qubits with dephasing bias η = pZ/pX >> 1), the XZZX surface code decouples pure dephasing noise into independent 1D repetition codes along diagonals, raising the dephasing threshold to 50%. Matching the suppressed bit-flip rate pX = p/(2η) requires an asymmetric rectangular patch with distance dZ substantially smaller than dX (e.g. dZ = 7–9 vs. dX = 33 at p = 0.1%), shrinking the physical qubit footprint by 3.4× to 4.25× compared to standard isotropic square codes:

Gidney's estimate stores most qubits in yoked surface codes (430 physical qubits each) and FeMoco's uses a hand-optimized floor plan; with plain patches throughout, our physical-qubit totals are upper bounds on those designs. The distance comparison is the like-for-like test of the error model.

10How it is checked

  • Engine. ftsim's dense and sparse backends agree on random circuits; 100 random circuits match Qiskit's statevector to 10⁻¹⁰; noisy outcome distributions match Aer's exact density-matrix results under the same channels, sampled plainly and stratified by fault count; the WebAssembly build returns the same records as the native one.
  • Programs. Adders, modular adders and multipliers verified on every input; Shor's noiseless outcomes match the exact phase-estimation distribution; every synthesized rotation verified as a matrix; phase estimation matches its exact Trotterized distribution.
  • Modern arithmetic. Full reversible uncomputation verified on all inputs; mid-circuit X measurements, patch resets, and classical feed-forward CZ fixups match unitaries.
  • Chemistry. Integrals, Hartree–Fock and FCI energies equal PySCF's to 10⁻⁸; Szabo & Ostlund's H₂ and HeH⁺ reproduced; tapering preserves the spectrum.
  • Calibration. stabilizer-qec against Stim + PyMatching; the reference-qubit experiment against plain memories; composition (Section 4); sensitivity analysis across physical noise bounds.
